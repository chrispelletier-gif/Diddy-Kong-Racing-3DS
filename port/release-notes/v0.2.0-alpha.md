**The performance update.** The second alpha of Diddy Kong Racing for Nintendo 3DS: an unofficial, native port built from the
community decompilation of the N64 game. It is about making the game run smoothly on every 3DS model, including the
heavy tracks at the sharpest settings. **It's still an early alpha.**

**No game files are included.** You need your own Diddy Kong Racing ROM, **US version 1.0** (SHA1
`0cb115d8716dbbc2922fda38e533b9fe63bb9670`).

## Installing

1. Copy your ROM to `sdmc:/3ds/dkr/baserom.z64`.
2. Either copy `dkr.3dsx` to `sdmc:/3ds/` and start it from the Homebrew Launcher, or install `dkr.cia` with FBI to get a
   HOME menu icon.
3. For sound, the DSP firmware dump `sdmc:/3ds/dspfirm.cdc` is needed (made with DSP1), as for other homebrew.

`SHA256SUMS.txt` has the files' checksums. The [README](https://github.com/chrispelletier-gif/Diddy-Kong-Racing-3DS#readme)
has the controls, settings and setup details. One download works on every model; the game picks its own settings for an
original 3DS, 3DS XL or 2DS.

## Upgrading from 0.1.0-alpha

Install over the old version. The first-run setup appears once to set up the new version, so take a minute to pick your
settings again. Your save files are not touched; it's still a good idea to back them up first (Settings > Saves).

## Performance: New 3DS and New 3DS XL

- **A steady 60 fps through the whole game.** A hands-free run through the menus, every kind of race, bosses, arenas and hub
  worlds (29 stops, 60 fps mode) averaged **59.6 fps** with a 1% low of 58 fps.
- **Two full 200-race test runs (400 races) at 800 px with adaptive anti-aliasing and widescreen** finished with no crashes:
  **59.8 fps on average, every race at 59 fps or better**, and no sound gaps. Heavy tracks like Crescent Island and Haunted
  Woods went from about 48 fps to 60.
- **Two-core renderer, on by default**, a third top-screen buffer, and a long list of renderer savings.
- **Adaptive anti-aliasing** drops anti-aliasing for a moment when the GPU can't keep up, instead of letting the frame rate sag.
- **Smoother hubs**: hub-world logic time dropped from 12.7 ms to 1.5 ms, which removes the frame drops in the hubs.
- **Fewer hitches and shorter pauses**: textures decode at load time, race and track loading is faster, and saving,
  logging, update checks and bug reports no longer stall the game (a 416 ms freeze when starting Adventure is gone). A texture
  memory leak that built up over long sessions is fixed.

## Performance: original 3DS, 3DS XL and 2DS

0.1 was tested on New 3DS only. In 0.2 the original-hardware family is playable.

- **About 60% faster than before:** a test of the five heaviest tracks went from **18.2 fps to 28.4 fps**.
- A **200-race test run finished 200 of 200 with no crashes**, no sound gaps and no texture failures: **29.2 fps on average
  against the 30 fps target, with 95% of frames at 30**. The slowest track, Whale Bay, still runs at 82% of frames at 30, and
  EverFrost Peak, Windmill Plains and Snowball Valley sit at 92 to 94%.
- How: a two-core renderer, dropping off-screen drawing early, simpler models for other karts at a distance, many renderer
  cuts, and defaults chosen for this hardware (30 fps, 2x anti-aliasing, Balanced widescreen). A steady 20 fps option is in the
  first-run setup for the worst case. 60 fps, 800 px mode and 4x anti-aliasing are New 3DS settings.
- Sound stays in time with the game, and the game keeps up through the HOME menu and sleep. The Jungle Falls tunnel pieces no
  longer blink out.

## Also in this release

- **Stereo 3D tuning** (Settings): 3D depth, convergence, HUD depth, and auto convergence with a comfort target, which eases the
  screen plane back when something close comes near, so the 3D stays comfortable. French labels included.
- **Tilt steering**: steer by tilting the console (best with the 3D slider off).
- **First-run setup**: language, play style (Classic, Enhanced, Speedy) and bottom-screen look, with a live preview of both
  screens. Run it again from Settings > Start-up.
- **Four bottom-screen looks**: Classic, Glass, Broadcast and Dock.
- **French** for the bottom screen and the game's menus.
- **Smoother camera at walls**: it eases in front of walls and back out instead of snapping.
- **Fixed: Diddy's head and cliff pieces flickering** on New 3DS. The GPU sometimes missed settings sent right after a draw;
  the game now writes each draw's settings twice (about 1.7 ms of GPU time on heavy tracks, which we judged worth it).
- Menu clean-up: no Controller Pak or Rumble Pak messages, N64 leftovers removed, SAVES / GHOSTS wording, Screen options
  and Intro logos in the game's Options menu, and bottom-screen lettering polish.

## How it was tested

The numbers above come from tools built into the **debug build** (`3ds-master-debug`; the player build leaves them out): a
hands-free AI driver that plays 200 races, a whole-game run through every mode, a switch bench that races one setting at a time,
a live performance monitor, a flight recorder that explains every hitch, in-game bug reports that post to GitHub, and a frame
comparison on every code change.

## Known issues

- Some original-3DS tracks still dip below 30 fps, Whale Bay most of all. Crowded race starts can dip too.
- A short pause (about a quarter of a second) when a race loads.
- The track-select screen can glitch when moving the cursor up and down.
- The Whale Bay hovercraft can get stuck in the automated test run; this does not affect normal play.
- Adventure mode hasn't been played end to end. One player only. US 1.0 ROM only.

## Reporting a problem

If the game crashes, it saves `last_crash.txt` and `last_log.txt` in `sdmc:/3ds/dkr/` the next time it starts. Please open an
issue saying what you were doing, which console you have and this version number, and attach those files.
